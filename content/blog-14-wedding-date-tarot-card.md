# The Card of the Day You Met

Plenty of couples pick a wedding date by its number. The method is easy to run and it's all over the wedding sites: add the digits of the full date, keep adding until one digit is left, and read what that digit means. A wedding on 14 June 2014 gives you 6 + 1 + 4 + 2 + 0 + 1 + 4, which comes to eighteen, and eighteen comes to nine. Nine is the number of completion, so the reading is that you're marrying at the close of something. The Knot runs a version of this. So do the numerology sites the wedding blogs borrow from, and they broadly agree with each other, because they're all running the same fold.

The fold is consistent, and people have been picking dates with it for decades. It also hands 14 June 2014 the same answer it gives to forty other days that year. The same fold sits under every birth-card calculator, where it costs the same thing, which is [why reduction can only reach half the deck](#).

## Your wedding date, kept whole

Tarot numerology can take a date and leave it whole. Add the digits of the year and stop there. 2014 comes to seven. Add the month, six, which gives thirteen. Add the day, fourteen, which gives twenty-seven. Then set that on [the wheel of twenty-two](#), the Major Arcana laid in a circle from zero to twenty-one and around again. Twenty-seven comes around to five.

Five is the Hierophant, and 14 June 2014 is a Hierophant day. Underneath him sits a Minor, the Three of Pentacles, its suit set by the Hierophant's element and its rank by the same date.

The Hierophant is an unexpected card to find under a wedding. He isn't romance. In the Almanac's reading of him, "he is tradition, lineage, the knowledge that gets handed down rather than invented from scratch. He stands for belonging to something older than yourself." A wedding is the most public borrowing of an old form that most people ever do. You say words other people wrote, in front of witnesses, because they were said before.

The two methods narrow at different rates. Forty-one days in 2014 fold to a nine. Thirteen of them carry the Hierophant. Two carry the Hierophant with the Three of Pentacles under him.

## Every anniversary is a different card

The wheel gives that same calendar date a different card every year you come back to it. So does the fold, for that matter. Both climb by one a year, because the only thing changing in the sum is the year, and both wrap when they run out of room. The difference is how much room there is.

The wedding is the Hierophant. The first anniversary is the Lovers, the second the Chariot, the third Strength, one step around the circle a year. Look down the folded column and it's doing the same walk with fewer places to stand. Nine years in, at the ninth anniversary in 2023, the fold is back on the nine it started with.

The wheel comes back to the Hierophant that year too, and it's a coincidence. Nine steps forward should land nine cards along. A decade turned in between, 2019 into 2020, and that one turn took eight off the total instead of adding one, which is a loss of exactly nine. The climb and the turn cancelled and the card came home.

Now look at 2029. The fold says six, the same six it said in 2020. The 2020s ran their whole length without a decade turn in them, so the wheel climbed all nine places and gave Justice.

Justice is card eleven. The fold's inventory runs one through nine, the Magician to the Hermit, and the other thirteen cards on the wheel sit outside it. The nine-year return is exact and it never breaks, so a couple married on 14 June gets nine folded numbers for as long as they keep the anniversary. Across the century between 1950 and 2050, that same date lands on all twenty-two Majors.

The Minor moves even when the Major repeats. The Hierophant came with the Three of Pentacles in 2014 and the Seven of Pentacles in 2023, and it'll come with the Page of Pentacles in 2032.

## The day you met, and the day you didn't choose

A wedding date is chosen. Most of the dates that matter aren't.

Say the two of you met on 8 October 2011. That day is the Fool, card zero, with the Five of Swords underneath. The Fool is the obvious card for a first meeting and you can enjoy it. The Five of Swords is a scrappier thing to find sitting under a beginning, and it was settled long before either of you turned up.

Nothing here is drawn or picked. The day you moved in, the day the job came through, the day someone was born, they all have their cards already, sitting in the arithmetic since before anyone thought to look. Some are lovely and some are hard. The hard ones are often the dates a couple can point to when they say this is when things changed.

## The date's card belongs to both of you

Your birth card is yours. So is [your Bearing](#), the number your birth month and birth day make, which never moves and which nobody born on another day carries.

A date's card is collective. Everyone alive on 14 June 2014 was under the Hierophant that day, the same way everyone born in one year shares [the card of the year they were born](#). Two people can stand under a date's card together. That's the one to write inside an anniversary card.

A personal column runs alongside it. Each of you reads that same date through your own Bearing, so the wedding hands each of you a card of your own as well. Those stay individual. The shared one is the anniversary's.

## Can you look up a date that hasn't happened yet?

[The dated pages here](#) run through the whole past and stop one month ahead of the current one. That's on purpose, and it means a wedding next spring doesn't have a page yet.

The arithmetic doesn't stop, though, and it's three steps. Say the wedding is 14 June 2027. Add the digits of the year: 2 + 0 + 2 + 7 is eleven. Add the month: eleven plus six is seventeen. Add the day: seventeen plus fourteen is thirty-one, which comes around past twenty-one to nine.

Nine is the Hermit, and 14 June 2027 is a Hermit day, whoever gets married on it. Check it against the page when the date comes near enough to open. It will say the Hermit, because there was never anywhere else for it to land.

The 2014 date reaches its twelfth anniversary this year, under Strength, which the Almanac reads as "power held quietly, the steadiness that can sit with fear, anger, and desire without being run by them." Twelve years in, that's a fair thing for a date to hand you.

Whatever your dates are, they were settled before you got there.
