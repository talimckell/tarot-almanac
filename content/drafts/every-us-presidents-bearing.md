# Every US President's Bearing

Your Bearing is one number: your birth month plus your birth day, run through the same mod-22 wheel the almanac runs everything through. The birth year drops out. What's left is the fixed distance between your card and the collective one, holding steady on every date, at every level, your whole life.

It's arithmetic, not shuffling, so I can run it on anyone with a known birthday. Forty-five people have had theirs written down more carefully than almost anyone alive. So I ran all of them, Washington through Trump. No birthdays invented, all public record. And because the Bearing drops the year, the usual birth-year squabbles don't touch it. Chester Arthur can keep lying about whether it was 1829 or 1830. His comes out the same either way.

Some of what came back is hard to look away from.

---

## A few that stopped me

### Donald Trump: Judgement

Judgement. Card twenty. The trumpet sounds, the graves open, everybody stands up to answer for what they actually did. It's the one card in the deck that's *about* the reckoning, and across two hundred and fifty years and forty-five presidents, exactly one lands on it.

The card about being called to account, and out of everyone who's ever held the office, it belongs to the one man spending his second term daring the country to hold him to anything at all.

### John Adams and John Quincy Adams: both The Moon

Father and son. Same office a generation apart, and the same Bearing down to the number.

The Moon reads the world by feel, trusts the gut over the polite version, and mistakes a feeling for a fact more often than it'll admit. Both Adamses were exactly that flavor of stubborn. Both were one-term presidents who left office furious and kept right on arguing, the son from a seat in Congress where he fought slavery harder than he ever fought for the job. Of all the cards the arithmetic could have handed a father and his son in common, it's the one that describes them both.

### Barack Obama: The Hanged One

The Hanged One is the one who gets everything by *not* forcing it. Sees the whole situation from upside down and calls that the strategy. No drama. The long game. Suspended above the room's noise on purpose and calling the suspension patience.

His band is ten, which is about as far from the collective card as a Bearing gets. Which is to say: he was almost never standing where the rest of the room was standing. Whatever you make of him, that reads true.

### Abraham Lincoln: Temperance

Temperance is the card of holding two things that badly want to fly apart and refusing to let either go. He did exactly that with an entire country for four years. His Bearing reading opens on two words, *You integrate.* He did, and it killed him. 💙

## What the whole spread says

Run all forty-five and the pile has a shape.

**Death and The Moon tie for most common, six presidents each.** The Death club is Polk, Taylor, Harding, Truman, Lyndon Johnson, and George W. Bush, which is a startlingly good list of men who transformed the country and didn't leave it where they found it, for better and for a lot of worse. The Moon club is the two Adamses, Jackson, Wilson, Hoover, and George H. W. Bush. Read-by-feel men, every one, and the card's shadow (feeling mistaken for fact) has a body count in that group.

**Nobody is the Emperor.** Not one, in the whole run, carries the Bearing of the father-king on the throne. Forty-five men who wanted to be the ultimate authority in the room, and not a single one drew the card of it.

**The closest to the collective, band of one, are Cleveland and Ford, both The World.** Two men the establishment considered whole and safe, one of them never elected to anything at either step. **The farthest out, band of eleven, are all Justice:** William Henry Harrison, Coolidge, and Carter. The ones who weighed things honestly and stood a long way off from the crowd to do it. One of them lasted 31 days. One said almost nothing. One lost an election for it and then spent forty years being right.

\* **A note on the old calendar.** Washington, John Adams, Jefferson, and Madison were all born before Britain switched from the Julian to the Gregorian calendar in 1752, so each has two birthdays on record. I used the dates the country actually observes (the New Style ones on every monument). It's worth knowing it's a real fork, because it moves the Bearing: by the old-calendar February 11th, Washington isn't the High Priestess at all. He's Death.

Every one of these came from nothing but a birthday and the same arithmetic. Yours works the exact same way.
