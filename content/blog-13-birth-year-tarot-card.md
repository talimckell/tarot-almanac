# The Card of the Year You Were Born

Add the digits of the year you were born. 1990 comes to nineteen: one, nine, nine, zero. Everyone born that year shares the number, and every version of this calculation starts there. The versions differ in what they do with it next.

## The fold, and the nine cards it reaches

The usual instruction is to keep going. Nineteen becomes ten, ten becomes one, and one is the Magician, so 1990 is a Magician year and anyone born in it gets the Magician. Match the final digit to the Major Arcana card wearing that number and you're finished. It's a consistent rule, and it's how most of the birth-year pages you'll find are built.

It's the same fold that governs [your personal year number](#), and it comes with the same inventory. A single digit runs one through nine, so the cards it can name are the Magician, the High Priestess, the Empress, the Emperor, the Hierophant, the Lovers, the Chariot, Strength, and the Hermit. Nine cards, the first nine steps of the deck, and everyone who has ever been born gets sorted onto one of them. That's [why reduction can only reach half the deck](#).

## Five years in the eighties, in order

Here's what the years do when you stop folding and set each sum on [the wheel of twenty-two](#), the Major Arcana laid in a circle from zero to twenty-one and around again.

1986 adds to twenty-four, which comes around to two, the High Priestess. 1987 adds to twenty-five, the Empress. 1988, the Emperor. 1989, the Hierophant. Each year adds one to the digit sum and moves one step around the circle, so the next one should be the Lovers.

1990 adds to nineteen, which is the Sun.

The eight and the nine at the end of 1989 turned into a nine and a zero, so that pair fell from seventeen to nine and took the year's total down with it, twenty-seven to nineteen. On a circle of twenty-two, falling eight and climbing fourteen arrive at the same place. The Sun sits thirteen cards past the Lovers.

1991 adds to twenty and gives Judgement. 1992 adds to twenty-one and gives the World, card twenty-one, the one the Fool's Journey spends its whole length climbing toward. 1993 runs the total to twenty-two, one past the end of the deck, so it comes around to zero and lands on the Fool. Then 1994 starts the climb again at the Magician.

Two people born a year apart, in 1989 and 1990, sit fourteen steps from each other on the wheel, the Hierophant and the Sun. Folded, they're a nine and a one, and neither of them gets the Sun.

## A century of birth years

Run it across a hundred years and the whole deck gets used. Every birth year from 1925 through 2025 lands somewhere on the wheel, and between them they reach all twenty-two Majors with none left over. Death is 1930. The Tower is 1933, 1942, 1951 and 1960. The Star, the Moon, the Sun, Judgement and the World all belong to real years with real people in them. The fold reaches nine of the twenty-two.

Two of the cards close. The Fool needs a digit sum of exactly twenty-two, which has happened seven times since 1900: 1939, 1948, 1957, 1966, 1975, 1984, 1993, every nine years. The World needs exactly twenty-one, and its last year was 1992. No year from 1994 to 2099 reaches either number. The late nineties overshoot and wrap past them, and the twenty-first century runs short, topping out at twenty in 2099. Anyone born under the Fool or the World carries a birth-year card that nobody born between 1994 and 2099 will have.

Death and the Hanged One are the thinnest rows in that century, one year apiece: 1930 for Death, 2019 for the Hanged One.

## The year card is shared

Your birth-year card is a collective card. You share it with everyone born in the same twelve months, and it says nothing about you in particular. [Your Bearing](#) is the personal one, made from your month and day, carried for life, and it leaves the year out on purpose. The two answer different questions.

The year card answers what the world was doing when you turned up. The Almanac reads it in [your natal chart](#) under the position called the world that caught you, and it's the one card in that chart you had no hand in. Somebody born in 1992, under the World, arrived in a year the deck calls arrival, "one of its rare moments of integration rather than striving." Somebody born eight days later, in 1993, arrived under the Fool, in "an open year, the world standing at zero with no clear map drawn."

The chart reads each of these cards with its shadow attached. The Sun's year gave its arrivals "a native faith in the possibility of gladness," and it also came with "the pressure to keep the light going regardless of what you actually feel."

## So is your birth-year card wrong?

If you've been carrying a Magician for 1990 and it fits, keep it. One digit and one card is a clean thing to hold, and plenty of people recognize themselves in it.

What the fold costs here is specific. Your birth year is the one number in your whole date that you had nothing to do with, chosen by nobody and shared with millions, and it's the closest the arithmetic comes to a record of the conditions you were born into. Reducing it to a digit between one and nine hands that record to nine cards and drops which of the twenty-two it actually was.

Your own sum is already sitting there in your birth year, and it points at one of them.
